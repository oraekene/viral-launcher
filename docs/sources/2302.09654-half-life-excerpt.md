# Vendored excerpt: the tweet half-life evidence

Source: Pfeffer, Matter, Sargsyan, "The Half-Life of a Tweet",
arXiv:2302.09654 [cs.SI], v2 11 Apr 2023.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2302.09654
Retrieved 2026-09-10 via arXiv abstract page and ar5iv HTML.
Full text and data: https://ar5iv.org/html/2302.09654 and
www.pfeffer.at/data/halflife.

This file exists because `half_life.minutes` is the only launcher
constant whose primary source was not vendored in-repo (#7). Short
quotes only; read the paper for the full analysis.

## What the paper found

- Median half-life of a tweet, defined as time until half of its
  24-hour impressions are created: **79.5 minutes** (mean 131.6).
  The launcher rounds to 80.
- Half-life quantiles: 10% at 7.2 min, 25% at 26.3 min,
  75% at 175.5 min, 90% at 342.1 min.
- Average impressions-per-second peaks **72 seconds** after posting.
- After 24 hours, ~95% of tweets gain no relevant further impressions;
  about one third of viewed tweets gain zero views on days 2-3.

## Verbatim lines relied upon

- "we estimate that the median half-life of a Tweet, i.e. the time it
  takes before half of all impressions are created, is about
  80 minutes." (abstract)
- "by ignoring a small number of very successful and long-lasting
  Tweets, we are able to define the median half-life of a Tweet with
  79.5 minutes." (conclusion)
- "Figure 5: Distribution of half-life values with mean = 131.6 min.
  (dashed line) and median = 79.5 min. (dotted line)."

## Caveats the paper states itself

- Short paper: data collection was limited by the ICWSM 2023 deadline;
  first dataset covers ~31.5 minutes per tweet, second ~11 hours.
- Tweets that go viral days or months later are explicitly out of scope.
- Sample: 21,685 tweets from January 2023, pre-algorithm-release regime;
  treat 80 minutes as a starting parameter, not a law. Calibration
  against own outcomes stays the convergence mechanism.
